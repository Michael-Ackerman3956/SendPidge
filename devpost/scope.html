SendPidge

An AI agent that, when a message claims to be family and asks for money, asks the real family member "was this you?" before the receiver acts.

The problem

Known-party impersonation works because verifying against the channel the receiver already has is possible but too costly per message, so it never happens.

A parent or grandparent gets a message claiming to be a family member and asking for money under pressure, and nothing on the platform can tell it from the real thing. She could check against the real person's number or thread she already has, but knowing to check, knowing how, and finding the nerve while the message says "hurry" costs more than trusting, so the check never happens. No platform can do it for her, because none holds a verified link to the real family member.

Full statement

A parent or grandparent receives an email or message that claims to be a family member and asks for money or a code under pressure ("my phone broke, use this number", "I'm at the police station"). The message looks ordinary: a clean address, passing authentication, nothing a spam filter or reputation score can flag, because a fresh account from a free provider carries no history. The receiver could confirm it, since they already hold something the impersonator doesn't (the real person's number, an old thread, another app), but that check almost never happens, so trust is extended by default to whoever shows up. Platforms stop at generic warnings because none of them holds a verified, consenting link between the receiver and the real family member, so none can ask him "was this you?" on the receiver's behalf, and scams move across email, SMS and messengers while each platform sees only its own. Verifying is therefore left to the receiver, and for them it costs three things each time: knowing that a check is needed at all, knowing how to do it (find the real number, call, compare the addresses), and the minutes and nerve to do it while the message says "hurry". The scammer never has to defeat a check; they only have to make it feel not worth doing, and the person paying that cost is the one least able to. The evidence is secondary, not first-hand: family-impersonation messages (메신저피싱) and AI voice-clone distress cases in Korea and the US. Investment scams are excluded because there is no known person to check against; calls are out of scope; email and messages are in.

The three costs the receiver pays

1. Knowing a check is needed

The message looks like every other one. Nothing flags it.

2. Knowing how

Find the real number, call, compare two addresses on a small screen.

3. Minutes and nerve

While the message says "hurry" and claims a child is in trouble.

The scammer never defeats a check. They make it feel not worth doing.

The unique kernel

The agent asks the real family member, on a channel only he controls, and shows his answer to the receiver. Not a score, not a warning: "Jimin replied, he did not send this." Delete that and it's another spam filter.

In the learner's words: while the receiver is looking at the email, the AI agent steps in, says it is running verification for a payment request, gets confirmation that this email was not sent from the real person, and shows "Phishing detected, Jimin replied, he did not send such email" with a button "Report and block". Grandma clicks it. Under a minute, on screen, recordable.

Boundary

Now (the proof of concept)
  • One receiver (Grandma), one trusted family member (Jimin), linked in advance
  • One inbound email that claims to be Jimin and asks for money
  • Detection: claims a known person + asks for money or a code
  • One question to the real Jimin on his channel, one yes/no answer
  • Grandma's screen: "checking", then the verdict with "Report and block"
  • Built on AWS; email in via SES
  • Open for PRD/spec: which channel carries the question to Jimin (email, SMS, WhatsApp). Round 1 recommended email-to-email first; no carrier or WhatsApp approval needed.
Later
  • Multiple family members; "which grandchild?" when no name is given
  • SMS and messenger inbound, not just email
  • Onboarding the family circle (consent flow) as a product surface
  • Institution impersonation ("fake police") as the known party
  • Business version (vendor bank-change, CEO wire request)
Explicitly cut
  • Investment and romance scams — no known person to ask; different root
  • Voice calls — out of scope by decision; can't be demoed credibly
  • Reputation or trust scores — structurally blind to fresh free-provider addresses
  • Link preview / URL safety — a different problem (link uncertainty), not this root
