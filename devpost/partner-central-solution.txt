=== External title ===
SendPidge: the safest trust layer for how people and organizations communicate

=== Value proposition (2–3 sentences) ===
SendPidge is the trust layer for the safest communication: it verifies who is really behind a message before a person or organization acts on it. A threat-intelligence engine of relationship graphs and threat lists holds the impersonation and asks the real sender. When fraud gets through, agentic flows run the recovery autonomously.

=== Description (max 5000 characters) ===
Most fraud begins with a message that looks like it comes from someone you trust: a grandchild who needs money today, a CEO asking for an urgent transfer, a vendor with new bank details, a regulator with a fine. Content filters can only guess from the words; only the real sender knows whether they sent it. The safest communication layer asks them, and treats every answer as intelligence for the next message.

Relationship graph. Each person or organization keeps a graph of who they know and the verified channels each contact controls: email, WhatsApp, RCS, in-app, and more as channels are added. A message that claims to come from someone in the graph but arrives from an address or number that person never used is the core signal, found in code before any model reads the text.

Threat intelligence. Threat lists of known-bad senders, lookalike identities (confusable names and domains checked against the receiver's own contacts), risky links and suspect payees, fed by every attack confirmed on the network and by curated commercial-licensed feeds. A match is a reason shown to the user, never a verdict on its own; the verdict comes from the real sender.

Preventive defense. Inbound mail arrives through Amazon SES Mail Manager with SPF and DKIM results stamped at the door. An Amazon Bedrock model reads the message with no tools and returns a structured verdict; code-based policy decides to pass, warn or hold, with a second-opinion escalation for borderline cases. A held message triggers "Was this you?" to the real sender on a channel they control (WhatsApp and RCS through AWS End User Messaging, or in-app), with a two-tap confirmation when money is involved. The AI can flag a message but never release one, and the user never sees the attacker's content until the sender has answered.

Reactive agentic flows. When fraud lands or money has already moved, an Amazon Bedrock AgentCore agent opens the case, drafts the bank report from the receiver's own records, follows up, and answers the user's questions, behind Amazon Bedrock Guardrails and with every tool scoped to the caller. The agent runs the recovery autonomously; the person stays in control of every outbound action.

Every organization that joins strengthens the whole: a confirmed attack against one member becomes a reason for every other, and verified channels become a shared way for banks, companies and platforms to reach the real person on the other side.

=== Step 2 · Integration details ===
SendPidge is open core: the engine is public code deployed with AWS CDK into the customer's own AWS account, where Amazon SES Mail Manager receives inbound email and stamps authentication results, Amazon Bedrock reads each message, Amazon Bedrock AgentCore runs the recovery agent behind Bedrock Guardrails, and AWS End User Messaging (WhatsApp and RCS) carries the "Was this you?" question to the real sender; state lives in Amazon DynamoDB and Amazon S3. The commercial layer is a hosted threat-intelligence API (threat lists and lookalike checks the deployed engine calls at verdict time) plus enterprise features for banks, insurers and carriers, who integrate through the same webhook and channel adapters to verify their own customers and vendors.

=== Step 3 · Use case details (max 500 each) ===
Use case 1: Identity Authentication
Impersonation works because a message cannot prove who sent it. SendPidge authenticates the sender, not the content: when a message claims a known contact but arrives from an unverified address, the real contact is asked on a channel they control via AWS End User Messaging, and only their answer releases it. Banks, insurers and carriers get a yes or no from the real person before any instruction is acted on.

Use case 2: Fraud Prevention
Authorised push payment and business email compromise losses keep rising because filters guess from content and the victim acts before anyone checks. SendPidge holds the message at the point of trust, before money moves, combining a relationship graph, threat lists and an Amazon Bedrock read with code-based policy. Every confirmed attack strengthens the threat lists for every other customer.

Use case 3: Incident Response
When fraud lands, the first hours decide whether money is recovered and most victims never report. An Amazon Bedrock AgentCore agent opens the case the moment an attack is confirmed, drafts the bank report from the customer's own records, follows up and answers the customer's questions, behind Bedrock Guardrails. The customer gets a case file instead of a hotline queue; the bank gets a complete report on day one.
